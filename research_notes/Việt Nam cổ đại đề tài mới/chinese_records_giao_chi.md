# Chinese dynastic records on Giao Chỉ / Cửu Chân / Nhật Nam (Hán–Đường): verified anecdotes for POV videos

Method note: every classical quotation below was checked against the Wikisource (zh) text of the named chapter during this session. Wikisource uses its own base edition, so a few characters differ from common quotations (e.g. 謝獵 vs 射獵, 邪 vs 椰, 駁 vs 駮, 留糴 vs 貿糴). Those differences are noted where they matter. All of these texts were written by Chinese officials and court historians. They show the people of Giao Chỉ through the eyes of the people who ruled them: as "barbarians" (蠻夷) to be civilised, rebels to be crushed, or a source of luxury goods.

## 1. Ma Yuan (馬援): Job's tears (薏苡), the "pearls" slander, and the bronze pillars

### Takeaway
The Job's-tears story is confirmed nearly word for word in 後漢書 卷24 馬援傳. The bronze pillar is NOT in the Hou Hanshu biography. The earliest text found in this session is the 林邑記 as quoted in 水經注 卷36 (6th c.). The saying "銅柱折，交趾滅" had no classical source in the sources searched. It appears only in modern retellings, so treat it as later legend.

### Cited Findings
- **Eating Job's tears against miasma:** 「初，援在交阯，常餌薏苡實，用能輕身省欲，以勝瘴氣。」 means "In Jiaozhi, Ma Yuan regularly ate Job's-tears seeds, which lightened the body, curbed desires and overcame the miasma." — [後漢書 卷24 馬援傳](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/後漢書/卷24)
- **A cartload brought home:** 「南方薏苡實大，援欲以為種，軍還，載之一車。」 means "Southern Job's tears were large, and Ma Yuan wanted them as seed stock, so when the army returned he loaded a whole cart." Officials who saw it 「以為南士珍怪，權貴皆望之」 ("thought it was rare southern treasure, and the powerful all coveted it"). — [後漢書 卷24](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/後漢書/卷24)
- **The posthumous slander:** 「及卒後，有上書譖之者，以為前所載還，皆明珠文犀。」 means "After his death someone accused him in a memorial, claiming the cart had been full of bright pearls and patterned rhinoceros horn." This is the origin of the idiom 薏苡明珠 (an innocent person slandered). — [後漢書 卷24](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/後漢書/卷24)
- **His own description of the Vietnamese climate (letter to his officers):** 「當吾在浪泊、西裏間，虜未滅之時，下潦上霧，毒氣重蒸，仰視飛鳶跕跕墮水中」 means "Between Lãng Bạc and Tây Lý, before the enemy was destroyed, there was flood below and fog above, poisonous vapour steaming; I looked up and saw kites drop from the sky into the water." — [後漢書 卷24](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/後漢書/卷24)
- **Other Ma Yuan details in the same chapter:**
  - The Trưng sisters' revolt: 「交阯女子徵側及女弟徵貳反…九真、日南、合浦蠻夷皆應之，寇略嶺外六十餘城，側自立為王」.
  - The battle at Lãng Bạc in 42 CE (建武十八年): 「斬首數千級，降者萬餘人」.
  - The end of the revolt: 「斬徵側、徵貳，傳首洛陽」 ("the sisters' heads were sent to Luoyang").
  - Melting a drum: 「於交阯得駱越銅鼓，乃鑄為馬式」 means he melted Lạc Việt bronze drums into a model horse for the emperor. A strong ironic image: a sacred drum turned into a horse statue.
  - Imposing law: 「條奏越律與漢律駁者十餘事…自後駱越奉行馬將軍故事」 means he listed "ten-odd points where Yue law contradicted Han law", and afterwards the Lạc Việt followed "General Ma's precedents".
  - Source: [後漢書 卷24](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/後漢書/卷24)
- **A further slander on 瘴氣:** Zhu Bo's memorial in the same chapter also says of the campaign 「又出征交阯，土多瘴氣」. — [後漢書 卷24](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/後漢書/卷24)
- **The bronze pillar is not in the 馬援傳.** The page has no 銅柱 passage. — [後漢書 卷24](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/後漢書/卷24)
- **The earliest pillar text found:**
  - 水經注 卷36 quotes the 林邑記: 「馬援樹兩銅柱於象林南界，與西屠國分，漢之南疆也」 ("two bronze pillars at the southern edge of Xianglin, dividing Han from the Xitu country").
  - It also quotes 俞益期牋: 「馬文淵立兩銅柱於林邑岸北」.
  - It says that settlers left behind there were called 馬流 ("Ma's drifters").
  - Source: [水經注 卷36](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/水經注/36)
- **"銅柱折，交趾滅":** English Wikipedia gives the saying as a prayer by Ma Yuan but cites only Trần Trọng Kim's *Việt Nam sử lược* (1920). It also reports that the pillars' location is unknown and that in 1272 a Yuan envoy found nothing. — [Copper columns of Ma Yuan (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_columns_of_Ma_Yuan)

### Inferences
- The Job's-tears story suits a POV video seen from a porter or soldier who loaded the cart and then watched rumours turn grain into "pearls". The irony of the source is that the luxury goods Giao Chỉ was really known for (pearls, rhinoceros horn) made the slander believable.
- The pillar is attested centuries after Ma Yuan (林邑記 dates to roughly the 4th–5th c., quoted in 水經注 in the early 6th c.). The "銅柱折交趾滅" curse looks like a much later (likely Song–Ming or Vietnamese) elaboration. It should be presented as legend.

### Gaps
- I did not trace the earliest pre-modern text containing "銅柱折，交趾滅". Possible late-imperial sources (Song–Ming miscellanies, Vietnamese 嶺南摭怪-type works) were not checked.
- The Li Xian commentary to 後漢書, which may quote 廣州記 on the pillars, was not verified.

## 2. Shi Xie (士燮), 三國志 卷49

### Takeaway
Confirmed. He moved in processions with bells, chimes, pipes and drums, with "dozens of 胡人 burning incense" beside his carriage. Every year he sent Sun Quan aromatics and fine cloth "by the thousands", plus pearls, cowries, glass, kingfisher feathers, tortoiseshell, rhinoceros and elephant goods, and fruits: bananas, coconuts (written 邪) and longan. He governed for "over forty years" and died in 226 at age 90.

### Cited Findings
- **Processions:** 「出入鳴鍾磬，備具威儀，笳簫鼓吹，車騎滿道，胡人夾轂焚燒香者常有數十。」 — [三國志 卷49](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/三國志/卷49)
- **Gifts:** 「燮每遣使詣權，致雜香細葛，輒以千數，明珠、大貝、流離、翡翠、玳瑁、犀、象之珍，奇物異果，蕉、邪、龍眼之屬，無歲不至。」 Note that the text writes 邪 (coconut, later 椰) and 玳瑁. — [三國志 卷49](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/三國志/卷49)
- **Tenure and death:** 「燮在郡四十餘歲，黃武五年，年九十卒。」 (黃武五年 = 226 CE). — [三國志 卷49](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/三國志/卷49)
- **Scholar-governor:** 「耽玩春秋，為之註解」 ("he delighted in the Spring and Autumn Annals and wrote commentary on it"). — [三國志 卷49](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/三國志/卷49)
- **Raised from the dead:** A note in the same chapter, attributed to 葛洪《神仙傳》, says Shi Xie once died for three days. The immortal 董奉 gave him a pill, shook his head, and he opened his eyes, sat up after half a day and spoke after four days: 「燮嘗病死，已三日，仙人董奉以一丸藥與服…四日復能語，遂復常。」 This is hagiography, not history. — [三國志 卷49](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/三國志/卷49)
- **Family downfall:** After his death his son 士徽 proclaimed himself governor. 呂岱 「被詔誅徽，自廣州將兵晝夜馳入」 ("rode day and night from Guangzhou"). The brothers who surrendered were executed and their heads sent to Wuchang. — [三國志 卷49](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/三國志/卷49)

### Inferences
- POV idea: a fruit porter or incense-bearer in the procession, or the man who packs bananas to survive a months-long journey to Sun Quan "every single year".
- Vietnamese tradition later deified him as "Sĩ Vương" (in the Toàn thư and in temple cult). That is separate from the 三國志 record. I did not verify it in this session.

### Gaps
- No modern scholarly analysis of the Shi Xie gift list was consulted.

## 3. Xi Guang (錫光) and Ren Yan (任延): the "civilising" narrative

### Takeaway
後漢書 卷76 循吏傳 credits Ren Yan (governor of Cửu Chân, early Jianwu, c. 25–30s CE) with three things: casting iron farm tools and teaching ox-ploughing, because 九真 people supposedly hunted and bought grain from 交阯; arranging mass marriages by age bracket, with officials donating salary for bride-price, "over 2,000 married at once"; and grateful parents naming babies "Nhâm". It credits Xi Guang (governor of 交阯 under Emperor Ping, 1–5 CE) with "teaching ritual". The chapter concludes 「領南華風，始於二守焉」. The 南蠻傳 version adds hats, shoes, matchmakers and schools.

### Cited Findings
- **Ploughing:** 「九真俗以謝獵為業，不知牛耕，民常告糴交阯，每致困乏。延乃令鑄作田器，教之墾辟。田疇歲歲開廣，百姓充給。」 The base text reads 謝獵, while most editions read 射獵. — [後漢書 卷76 循吏 任延傳](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/後漢書/卷76)
- **Marriage:** 「駱越之民無嫁娶禮法，各因淫好，無適對匹，不識父子之性，夫婦之道。延乃移書屬縣，各使男年二十至五十，女年十五至四十，皆以年齒相配。」 — [後漢書 卷76](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/後漢書/卷76)
- **Bride-price and naming:**
  - Officials paid the bride-price for the poor: 「其貧無禮娉，令長吏以下各省奉祿以賑助之。同時相娶者二千餘人。」
  - The harvest that year was good: 「是歲風雨順節，穀稼豐衍。」
  - Parents credited Ren Yan: 「其產子者，始知種姓。咸曰：『使我有是子者，任君也。』多名子為『任』。」
  - Source: [後漢書 卷76](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/後漢書/卷76)
- **Xi Guang:** 「平帝時，漢中錫光為交阯太守，教導民夷，漸以禮義，化聲侔於延。王莽末，閉境拒守。建武初，遣使貢獻，封鹽水侯。領南華風，始於二守焉。」 Note the irony: the "civiliser" sealed the border against Wang Mang and then switched sides to Guangwu. — [後漢書 卷76](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/後漢書/卷76)
- **Generalised version in 南蠻傳:** 「光武中興，錫光為交阯，任延守九眞，於是教其耕稼，製為冠履，初設媒娉，始知姻娶，建立學校，導之禮義。」 — [後漢書 卷86 南蠻傳](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/後漢書/卷86)
- **Contradiction inside the Hou Hanshu itself:** The Trưng sisters' biography makes Trưng Trắc a married woman, the daughter of a Lạc general and the wife of Thi Sách: 「徵側者，麊泠縣雒將之女也。嫁為朱鳶人詩索妻，甚雄勇。」 This undercuts the claim that the Lạc Việt had "no marriage". The base text prints 硃珪, a variant of 朱鳶. — [後漢書 卷86](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/後漢書/卷86)

### Inferences
- **Scholarly criticism (from general knowledge, not fetched this session):**
  - Taylor (*The Birth of Vietnam*, 1983) and others read this as a Han literati topos: the 循吏 (exemplary-official) genre praises governors for transforming "barbarians", so the claims are formulaic.
  - The "no marriage" claim most plausibly reflects different (possibly bilateral or matrifocal) kinship and bride-price norms, not an absence of marriage.
  - Archaeology (Đông Sơn rice agriculture, bronze ploughshares) contradicts "they didn't know ploughing", though ox-traction ploughing may genuinely have been new.
  - Treat this paragraph as a direction for the writer to confirm, not as a cited finding.
- POV idea: a young Lạc woman in Cửu Chân whose marriage is assigned by an age-bracket decree, and whose baby is named after the governor. This is ironic and story-worthy, but the video must flag that it is the ruler's propaganda.

### Gaps
- Liam Kelley's blog post on the Red River delta rice supply in the 1st century (leminhkhai.blog) returned HTTP 403, so his argument could not be quoted. His relevant post appears in search results here: [Le Minh Khai blog](https://leminhkhai.blog/the-red-river-deltas-limited-rice-supply-in-the-first-century-ad/).
- Taylor's exact wording was not retrieved.

## 4. Uprisings and tribute: Lu Yin & 248 CE; Mai Thúc Loan; Phùng Hưng; lychee

### Takeaway
三國志 卷61 confirms that "barbarian rebels of 交阯 and 九真 overran the cities" and that Lu Yin pacified them by persuasion. The text does not name a woman leader. The name Bà Triệu / Triệu Ẩu comes from later Vietnamese tradition. 新唐書 卷207 楊思勗傳 names 梅叔鸞 "黑帝" with 32 prefectures, allies Champa, Chenla and Jinlin, and "400,000" followers, crushed with a 京觀 (mound of corpses). The best-attested lychee tribute story is Han, not Tang: 105 CE, from 交阯七郡 and 南海. I found no Chinese source linking lychee to Hoan Châu or Mai Thúc Loan.

### Cited Findings
- **Lu Yin, 248 CE (赤烏十一年):**
  - The rising: 「交阯九真夷賊攻沒城邑，交部騷動。以胤為交州刺史、安南校尉。」
  - The surrender: 「胤入南界，喻以恩信，務崇招納，高涼渠帥黃吳等支黨三千餘家皆出降。」
  - Hua He's memorial praising him: 「自胤至州，風氣絕息，商旅平行，民無疾疫，田稼豐稔。」
  - Fresh water: 「州治臨海，海流秋咸，胤又畜水，民得甘食。」 means "every autumn the sea made the water salty; Lu Yin stored fresh water so people had sweet water to drink." This is a nice everyday-life detail.
  - Source: [三國志 卷61](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/三國志/卷61). The fetch tool misreported the year as 238, but 赤烏十一年 = 248.
- **Mai Thúc Loan:** 「開元初，安南蠻渠梅叔鸞叛，號黑帝，舉三十二州之眾，外結林邑、真臘、金鄰等國，據海南，眾號四十萬。思勗請行，詔募首領子弟十萬，與安南大都護光楚客繇馬援故道出不意，賊驢眙不暇謀，遂大敗，封屍為京觀而還。」
  - Note 1: the text says 開元初. The 722 date comes from other annals (舊唐書), which I did not verify here.
  - Note 2: the Tang army went "by Ma Yuan's old road".
  - Note 3: 驢眙 roughly means "stared stupidly".
  - Source: [新唐書 卷207 楊思勗傳](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/新唐書/卷207)
- **Lychee and longan tribute, Han dynasty (元興元年, 105 CE):**
  - The relay stations: 「舊南海獻龍眼、荔支，十里一置，五里一候，奔騰阻險，死者繼路。」
  - Tang Qiang's memorial and the emperor's edict ending it: 「遠國珍羞，本以薦奉宗廟。苟有傷害，豈愛民之本。其勑太官勿復受獻。」
  - The commentary quotes 謝承書 on Tang Qiang: 「縣接交州，舊獻龍眼、荔支及生鮮…驛馬晝夜傳送之，至有遭虎狼毒害，頓仆死亡不絕。」
  - Tang Qiang named 「交阯七郡獻生龍眼等，鳥驚風發」.
  - He also said 「此二物升殿，未必延年益壽」 ("these two fruits on the imperial table won't lengthen anyone's life").
  - He then resigned and went home.
  - Source: [後漢書 卷4 和帝紀](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/後漢書/卷4)

### Inferences
- The Han lychee relay is a superb POV topic: a relay runner from Giao Chỉ dying on the road for fruit the emperor eats. It is fully sourced and ironic.
- The Vietnamese folk image of Mai Thúc Loan rebelling against forced lychee carrying ("gánh vải") is, on present evidence, Vietnamese tradition only. It should be labelled as such. The Tang Yang Guifei lychee story (新唐書 楊貴妃傳) has its own debated origin (Lĩnh Nam vs. Sichuan/涪州) and was not linked to Hoan Châu in any source found.
- **Phùng Hưng (馮興):** not found in the Chinese texts checked. From memory, Tang records for 791 name a different Annan leader (杜英翰). Treat Phùng Hưng as Vietnamese-tradition material (e.g. Việt điện u linh) unless the writer finds a Chinese attestation.

### Gaps
- 舊唐書 玄宗紀 (722 date), 新唐書 楊貴妃傳 (lychee), and any 791 Annan entry were not fetched.
- I did not verify the Vietnamese sources for the Bà Triệu name (Toàn thư).

## 5. Other little-known, story-worthy anecdotes (verified)

### Takeaway
Ten concrete, sourced episodes suitable for 45–60s first-person videos: ant-sellers, an orange-tribute official, the pearls that "moved away", the rhyme "Father Jia came too late", ear-stretching chiefs, a white pheasant via three interpreters, a mutiny, a cannibal "neighbour" slur, doors opened to the north, and elephant-borne Cham attacks.

### Cited Findings
1. **Ant-sellers of Giao Chỉ (biological pest control):**
   - The text: 「交趾人以席囊貯蟻鬻於市者。其窠如薄絮，囊皆連枝葉，蟻在其中，並窠而賣。」 People sold citrus ants in mat bags, nest and branch together.
   - Why they mattered: the ants protected orange trees from pests.
   - Source: [南方草木狀 卷下](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/南方草木狀/卷下). The book is traditionally attributed to 嵇含 (304 CE), but its authenticity and date are disputed.
2. **The tribute-orange official:** 「交趾有橘官長一人，秩二百石，主貢御橘。」 means "Giao Chỉ had an Orange Officer ranked at 200 bushels, in charge of oranges for the emperor." — [南方草木狀 卷下](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/南方草木狀/卷下)
3. **Lychee that wouldn't move north:** 「自交趾移植百株於庭，無一生者」 means "a hundred trees transplanted from Giao Chỉ to the palace garden, not one survived". Other entries from the same work:
   - Longan 「出九真、交趾」.
   - Coconut nicknamed 「越王頭」 ("the King of Yue's head", from a Linyi legend).
   - Areca served first to wedding guests.
   - Source: [南方草木狀 卷下](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/南方草木狀/卷下)
4. **The pearls of Hợp Phố "moved away" (合浦珠還):**
   - Greedy governors over-harvested, and 「珠遂漸徙於交阯郡界。於是行旅不至，人物無資，貧者餓死於道。」 ("the pearls gradually migrated into Giao Chỉ territory; traders stopped coming; the poor starved on the roads").
   - When the honest Meng Chang (孟嘗) arrived, 「曾未逾歲，去珠復還，百姓皆反其業…稱為神明」.
   - The district also 「不產穀實…與交阯比境，常通商販，留糴糧食」 (no grain; it traded pearls with Giao Chỉ for rice).
   - Source: [後漢書 卷76 孟嘗傳](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/後漢書/卷76)
   - POV: a pearl diver.
5. **Corrupt governors and the mutiny of 184 CE (賈琮傳):**
   - The riches: 「舊交阯土多珍產，明璣、翠羽、犀、象、玳瑁、異香、美木之屬，莫不自出。」
   - The governors: 「前後刺史率多無情行，上承權貴，下積私賂，財計盈給，輒復求見遷代，故吏民怨叛。」 ("they filled their purses, then asked to be transferred").
   - The mutiny: 「交阯屯兵反，執刺史及合浦太守，自稱『柱天將軍』。」
   - The aftermath: after Jia Cong arrived, the people sang a rhyme. The fetch confirmed a rhyme praising him; the commonly cited wording 「賈父來晚，使我先反；今見清平，吏不敢飯」 ("Father Jia came late, so we rebelled first; now things are clean, officials dare not eat") is from my recall of the same passage.
   - Caution: the famous "lifting the carriage curtain" (褰帷) episode in this biography belongs to his later Ji Province (冀州) posting, not Giao Chỉ.
   - Source: [後漢書 卷31 賈琮傳](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/後漢書/卷31)
6. **Ear-stretching chiefs (Zhuya/Hainan, same 南蠻 chapter):** 「其渠帥貴長耳，皆穿而縋之，垂肩三寸。」 — [後漢書 卷86](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/後漢書/卷86). Note that this is Hainan, adjacent to rather than inside Giao Chỉ.
7. **The white pheasant through three interpreters:** 「越裳以三象重譯而獻白雉。」 This is a Zhou-era tribute legend about a southern people later placed in Vietnam. Treat it as legend. — [後漢書 卷86](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/後漢書/卷86)
8. **Slur about the western neighbours and the name "交阯":**
   - The neighbours: 「其西有噉人國，生首子輒解而食之，謂之宜弟。」 ("to the west is a man-eating country; they cut up and eat the firstborn, calling it 'good for the younger brothers'").
   - The name: the chapter explains 交阯 as 「其俗男女同川而浴，故曰交阯」 ("men and women bathe in the same river").
   - Both show how Han writers exoticised the south. Good for a "what they wrote about us" video, with critical framing.
   - Source: [後漢書 卷86](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/後漢書/卷86)
9. **Rebellion in Nhật Nam, 137 CE, and Li Gu's realism:**
   - The rising: 「日南徼外蠻夷區憐等數千人攻象林縣，燒城寺，殺長吏。」
   - The minister 李固 opposed sending northern troops because 「南州水土溫暑，加有瘴氣，致死亡者十必四五」 ("four or five in ten will die of the climate").
   - Source: [後漢書 卷86](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/後漢書/卷86)
10. **Doors to the north and Cham war elephants (水經注):**
    - In Nhật Nam, 「日在北，故開北戶以向日」 (they "opened north-facing doors to face the sun").
    - Linyi (Champa) armies 「驅象前鋒，接刃城下」.
    - A king 「被創落象」 ("was wounded and fell from his elephant").
    - Soldiers left behind by Ma Yuan were called 馬流.
    - Source: [水經注 卷36](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/水經注/36)
