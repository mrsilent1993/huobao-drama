Several numbers below come from the Archaeology magazine feature on Nam C. ## 1. Cổ Loa: the Cầu Vực arrowhead hoard (1959), the Cổ Loa drum hoard (Mả Tre, 1982), and the ramparts

### Takeaway
The Cầu Vực find was a ~1 m square pit holding 93 kg of bronze arrowheads, "gần một vạn chiếc" (close to 10,000) — NOT "hàng vạn" (tens of thousands); about three quarters were fresh, unfinished castings, i.e. a workshop stockpile, not a battlefield. The Mả Tre drum (Cổ Loa I) was found on 21 June 1982 by teenagers lowering a paddy field and held 200+ bronze objects, overwhelmingly farm tools (143 ploughshares per the heritage-site inventory; Archaeology magazine says 96). Cổ Loa has three rampart rings (outer ~8 km, middle ~6.5 km / "four miles", inner ~1.6 km / "slightly over a mile").

### Cited Findings
**Cầu Vực arrowheads (June 1959)**
- [ARCH] In June 1959 road-building workers at Cầu Vực (Cổ Loa) found a near-square pit "khoảng 1m" per side, "sâu khoảng 1,2 m", containing "93 kg" of bronze arrowheads, estimated at "gần một vạn chiếc" — [Nhân Dân, TS Lại Văn Tới](https://nhandan.vn/mui-ten-dong-co-loa-tu-huyen-thoai-den-lich-su-post504549.html)
- [ARCH] Conflicting figures: VnExpress reports "hàng vạn chiếc" and "gần 110 kg" — [VnExpress](https://vnexpress.net/mui-ten-ba-canh-cua-no-than-lien-chau-2416187.html); Archaeology magazine (2016) says "a cache of 10,000 bronze-tanged projectiles near the southern entrance" — [Hilgers, Archaeology Jul/Aug 2016, PDF](https://www.anthropology.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/NCKim-Hilgers_VNs-First-City-Arch-Mag_2016.pdf). **Safest wording: "about 10,000 arrowheads, ~93 kg" (Institute of Archaeology author).**
- [ARCH] Seven size types, longest 11 cm, shortest 6 cm; three-part form (tip, body, tang), all triangular in cross-section ("mũi tên ba cạnh") — [Nhân Dân](https://nhandan.vn/mui-ten-dong-co-loa-tu-huyen-thoai-den-lich-su-post504549.html)
- [ARCH] About one quarter had been reworked/finished; ~three quarters were fresh from the mould, unused, still bearing casting seams. No cultural layer or other artefacts in the pit → [INTERP] a storage pit of arrowheads awaiting finishing — [Nhân Dân](https://nhandan.vn/mui-ten-dong-co-loa-tu-huyen-thoai-den-lich-su-post504549.html); same "one quarter finished" point in [Kiến Thức](https://kienthuc.net.vn/di-san/tan-mat-nhin-bang-chung-lich-su-ve-no-than-an-duong-vuong-2011320.html)
- [ARCH] Excavations at Đền Thượng (Cổ Loa) in 2004–2007 found furnaces and hundreds of stone moulds; moulds were three-piece ("ba mang"), each piece forming one edge of the arrowhead; some intact, carved with arrowhead shapes; plus stone raw material for moulds — [Nhân Dân](https://nhandan.vn/mui-ten-dong-co-loa-tu-huyen-thoai-den-lich-su-post504549.html)
- [ARCH] Matching arrowheads also from Bãi Mèn, Đồng Vông, Đường Thụt, Đường Mây, Đình Tràng and Cổ Loa hamlets Xóm Nhồi, Xóm Hương, Xóm Gà, Xóm Mít, Xóm Vang — [Nhân Dân](https://nhandan.vn/mui-ten-dong-co-loa-tu-huyen-thoai-den-lich-su-post504549.html)
- [LEGEND] Link to An Dương Vương's magic crossbow (nỏ thần, móng rùa vàng / Kim Quy) — the arrowheads are routinely tied to it in press, but the hoard itself only proves mass arrowhead production — [Archaeology 2016](https://www.anthropology.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/NCKim-Hilgers_VNs-First-City-Arch-Mag_2016.pdf)

**Mả Tre / Cổ Loa drum hoard**
- [ARCH] 21 June 1982: teenagers helping the household of Tai Kim Quang lower paddy plots at Mả Tre (a former cemetery SW of Cổ Loa's South Gate) found a bronze drum ~30 cm below the surface, lying upside-down ("on its back"), covered in yellow clay, containing 200+ objects — [Hoàng Thành Thăng Long heritage centre](https://hoangthanhthanglong.vn/en/ma-tre-relic-co-loa-commune/)
- [ARCH] Inventory per that page: 247 pieces + 19.5 kg of broken bronze scrap. Production tools 194 (143 ploughshares, 34 axes, 8 picks, 4 shovels, 2 chisels, 2 harvesting knives, 1 knife); weapons 29 (16 spears, 5 daggers, 8 arrowheads); vessels 7 (incl. 3 broken jars, 1 basin, 1 tray); musical instruments 3 (whole drum, broken drum, 1 rattle/bell); 15 unidentified — [Hoàng Thành Thăng Long](https://hoangthanhthanglong.vn/en/ma-tre-relic-co-loa-commune/)
- [ARCH, CONFLICT] Archaeology magazine gives "1980s"/"1981", drum "158 pounds [~72 kg], 29 inches across [~74 cm], 21 inches tall [~53 cm]", contents "96 plowshares, six hoes, and a variety of axes, daggers, and arrowheads", plus "a Chinese coin from 200 B.C. and an inscription in Chinese on the inner surface" — [Archaeology 2016](https://www.anthropology.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/NCKim-Hilgers_VNs-First-City-Arch-Mag_2016.pdf). Date (1981 vs 1982) and ploughshare count (96 vs 143) conflict; Vietnamese heritage source is likely the more detailed inventory. Drum dimensions not confirmed by a Vietnamese source here.
- [INTERP] The scrap bronze suggests Mả Tre was a collection point of raw metal for a large casting batch — [Nhân Dân](https://nhandan.vn/mui-ten-dong-co-loa-tu-huyen-thoai-den-lich-su-post504549.html); one Vietnamese paper describes Cổ Loa as a metallurgy centre with arrowhead workshop at Đền Thượng, ploughshare casting at Mả Tre, products stored at Cầu Vực — [Nguyễn Thị Thảo Giang, Thông báo khoa học BTLSQG 2014](https://baotanglichsu.vn/DataFiles/asset/upload/data_hung_/thong_bao_khoa_hoc/so_1_nam_2014/Dinh_Trang_trong_moi_quan_he..._Nguyen_Thi_Thao_Giang.pdf)
- [ARCH] Drum surface: circular bands of warriors, drummers and "people threshing rice" — [Archaeology 2016](https://www.anthropology.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/NCKim-Hilgers_VNs-First-City-Arch-Mag_2016.pdf)

**Ramparts**
- [ARCH] Three spiraling packed-earth walls. Middle wall (irregular) "runs for four miles [~6.4 km] and stands about 30 feet [~9 m]"; inner wall roughly rectangular, "slightly over a mile" (~1.6 km); outer wall "five-mile perimeter" (~8 km), today only 10–12 ft high — [Archaeology 2016](https://www.anthropology.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/NCKim-Hilgers_VNs-First-City-Arch-Mag_2016.pdf). Area enclosed by outer wall "maybe 500 to 600 hectares" (Kim quote) — same source; Wikipedia gives ~600 ha, 8 km circumference, ramparts up to 12 m high and 25 m wide at base — [Wikipedia (pointer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%E1%BB%95_Loa_Citadel)
- [ARCH] Kim & Trinh Hoang Hiep's 2006+ trench through the middle wall near the north gate: three phases; earliest in 4th c. BCE (radiocarbon), middle phase 300–100 BCE; first phase "just piled earth", middle layers rammed earth (Chinese-like technique) — [Archaeology 2016](https://www.anthropology.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/NCKim-Hilgers_VNs-First-City-Arch-Mag_2016.pdf); formal publication: Kim, Lai Van Toi & Trinh Hoang Hiep 2010, "Co Loa: an investigation of Vietnam's ancient capital", Antiquity 84(326):1011–1027 (cited via [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citadel_of_C%E1%BB%95_Loa); not read directly)
- [INTERP] Middle-phase soil moved >30 million cubic feet; >5 million person-workdays (5-hour workday) for the early rampart system; built in "years or decades", not centuries — Kim, [Archaeology 2016](https://www.anthropology.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/NCKim-Hilgers_VNs-First-City-Arch-Mag_2016.pdf)
- [ARCH] A walkway-like depression ran along the top of an early phase; a later layer was full of ceramic roof tiles → [INTERP] a roof along the rampart or tiles to stop erosion. Roof tiles bear starburst patterns matching Đông Sơn bronze motifs. Ditches were V-shaped in section (dry military ditch, later perhaps water channels); a reservoir inside still floods "like a lake"; pollen suggests the area was heavily forested — same source
- [ARCH] Kilns, decorated roof tiles, weapons and large-scale bronze workshops found inside the walls — same source

### Inferences
- Story angle: the "magic crossbow" arrows were mass-produced in stone three-piece moulds — 3 out of 4 never shot, still with seams. A worker's-eye "factory floor" POV is better supported than a battle POV.
- The drum used as a storage bin, packed with ploughshares and scrap and buried upside-down, is a vivid "treasure buried in a hurry / metal bank" image (reason for burial unknown).

### Gaps
- Exact count of Cầu Vực arrowheads: no full count found; ~10,000 is an estimate from weight.
- Couldn't confirm the Cổ Loa drum dimensions/weight or the Chinese inscription from a Vietnamese museum source (BTLSQG site down). Year 1981 vs 1982 unresolved (heritage centre gives exact date 21/6/1982).
- Antiquity 2010 paper not read directly; rampart lengths come from popular-press summary.

## 2. Tooth blackening, betel chewing, tattooing

### Takeaway
Tooth blackening now has direct chemical evidence: a 2026 study of Đông Xá (Red River delta, Iron Age ~550 BCE–50 CE) found iron and sulfur in stained enamel, the signature of an iron-salt + tannin paste, i.e. deliberate blackening, not just betel staining. Tattooing for the Lạc Việt rests only on Chinese textual stereotypes of the "Yue" (文身斷髮); no physical evidence was found.

### Cited Findings
- [ARCH] Study: Zhang, Wang, Nguyen, Iizuka & Hung (2026), Archaeological and Anthropological Sciences, DOI 10.1007/s12520-025-02366-5; site Đông Xá (Dong Xa), late Đông Sơn, settlement/cemetery ~550 BCE–50 CE — [Archaeology magazine news 13 Feb 2026](https://archaeology.org/news/2026/02/13/cosmetic-tooth-blackening-dates-back-2000-years-in-vietnam/); [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/archaeology/2-000-year-old-skulls-reveal-people-in-ancient-vietnam-permanently-blackened-their-teeth-a-stylish-practice-that-persists-today)
- [ARCH] Enamel showed elevated iron and sulfur; researchers stained a modern animal tooth with a recreated iron-tannin mixture as a control and got a matching elemental pattern — [Gizmodo summary](https://gizmodo.com/2000-year-old-skulls-reveal-how-vietnamese-trendsetters-kept-their-teeth-pitch-black-2000725323); [Archaeology](https://archaeology.org/news/2026/02/13/cosmetic-tooth-blackening-dates-back-2000-years-in-vietnam/)
- [INTERP] Paste likely made by heating iron salts with tannin-rich plant extract and applied repeatedly over several weeks — [Archaeology](https://archaeology.org/news/2026/02/13/cosmetic-tooth-blackening-dates-back-2000-years-in-vietnam/)
- [ARCH/INTERP] Betel chewing alone gives reddish-brown staining; black iron-rich enamel points to a deliberate cosmetic. Altered dental colour affects at least one-fifth of individuals in the wider Đông Sơn assemblage (other studies ~40%). Possible motives: milder substitute for tooth ablation; or to enhance betel staining; spread linked to Iron Age availability of iron — reported via press summaries ([Ecoticias](https://www.ecoticias.com/en/2000-year-old-skulls-in-vietnam-reveal-that-ancient-communities-permanently-blackened-their-teeth-a-stylish-practice-that-survived-long-after-the-people-who-wore-it-disappeared/31754/); [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/archaeology/2-000-year-old-skulls-reveal-people-in-ancient-vietnam-permanently-blackened-their-teeth-a-stylish-practice-that-persists-today)) [WEAK — secondary press, numbers not checked in paper]
- [TEXT] Classical Chinese descriptions of southern Yue peoples: "文身斷髮" (tattooed bodies, cropped hair); Baiyue characterised by short hair, tattoos, fine swords, naval skill — [Lạc Việt (Wikipedia mirror, pointer)](https://ipfs.ssi.eecc.de/ipns/en.wikipedia-on-ipfs.org/wiki/L%E1%BA%A1c_Vi%E1%BB%87t); [Baiyue](https://encycloreader.org/r/wikipedia.php?q=Baiyue)

### Inferences
- Strong POV: a teen getting teeth blackened over weeks with a stinging iron-tannin paste; the chemistry is now testable fact, the ritual meaning is interpretation.
- Tooth blackening as a deliberate fashion predates Chinese rule (on these dates), which counters the idea it was a later custom.

### Gaps
- Number of Đông Xá individuals/teeth analysed not obtained (paper not read).
- I did not locate verified primary passages for 黑齒/雕題 or for the oft-quoted story (Hanshu/Shuijing zhu tradition) that the Lạc Việt tattooed themselves against "giao long" water dragons; that story also appears in Lĩnh Nam chích quái (Hùng king advising tattoos) — treat as [LEGEND/TEXT] until checked against the original texts.
- No direct archaeological evidence (preserved skin, tattooing tools) of Đông Sơn tattooing found.
- Direct biomolecular betel evidence for Đông Sơn specifically not found in this session.

## 3. Key objects: Ngọc Lũ drum, Đào Thịnh jar, Làng Vạc, Việt Khê boat-coffin

### Takeaway
Each object gives a different window: Ngọc Lũ (ritual scenes), Đào Thịnh (fertility couples on the lid), Làng Vạc (daggers with female-figure handles, beads, elephant-rider lamp), Việt Khê (a log-coffin "boat" packed with 107 grave goods including a drum).

### Cited Findings
**Ngọc Lũ drum (trống đồng Ngọc Lũ I)**
- [ARCH] Found c. 1893–1894 at Ngọc Lũ village, Lý Nhân (Hà Nam); now in Bảo tàng Lịch sử Quốc gia; diameter ~79 cm, height ~63 cm — [VnExpress](https://vnexpress.net/bao-vat-quoc-gia-doc-dao-2244776.html); [Kiến Thức](https://kienthuc.net.vn/kho-tri-thuc/nhung-bao-vat-quoc-gia-ma-nguoi-viet-nam-nao-cung-biet-den-1613627.html)
- [WEAK] Weight 86 kg — only [mytour.vn blog](https://mytour.vn/vi/blog/bai-viet/trong-dong-ngoc-lu-i.html)
- [CAUTION] A modern replica "nặng gần 4 tấn" (2.35 m diameter) is NOT the original — [Kiến Thức](https://kienthuc.net.vn/di-san/ngam-trong-dong-ngoc-lu-nang-gan-4-tan-lon-nhat-viet-nam-1941944.html)
- [ARCH] Cổ Loa drum shows a procession like Ngọc Lũ's but with only two spear-bearing warriors — [Bronze drum (Wikipedia pointer)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_drum)

**Đào Thịnh jar (thạp đồng Đào Thịnh)**
- [ARCH] Found by chance on the bank of the Red River at Đào Thịnh commune, Trấn Yên, Yên Bái; year given as 1960 or 1961 (sources disagree); bronze with lid; height 98 cm, mouth diameter 61 cm; National Treasure — [Kiến Thức](https://kienthuc.net.vn/kho-tri-thuc/doi-song-nguoi-viet-co-qua-nhung-buc-tuong-hon-2000-nam-tuoi-1992845.html); [VnExpress](https://vnexpress.net/bao-vat-quoc-gia-doc-dao-2244776.html)
- [ARCH] Lid carries four pairs of figures in sexual union at the four corners; men wear loincloth (khố) with dagger at hip, women short skirts — [Kiến Thức](https://kienthuc.net.vn/kho-tri-thuc/doi-song-nguoi-viet-co-qua-nhung-buc-tuong-hon-2000-nam-tuoi-1992845.html) — [INTERP] fertility cult ("tín ngưỡng phồn thực")

**Làng Vạc (Nghĩa Đàn, Nghệ An)**
- [ARCH] Discovered 1972 (or 1973; sources differ); Đông Sơn site in the Cả (Lam) river basin; bronze, pottery, stone, glass and iron artefacts; bronze daggers most numerous, some with T-shaped hilts, some with hilts shaped as a woman; 34 bead-string objects (stone, glass, terracotta) in Nghệ An museum — [Báo Nghệ An](https://baonghean.vn/en/bao-vat-duy-nhat-co-o-xu-nghe-10089206.html); [Báo Văn hóa](https://baovanhoa.vn/van-hoa/bao-ton-phat-huy-gia-tri-lich-su-van-hoa-di-tich-khao-co-hoc-lang-vac-31923.html)
- [ARCH] Bronze lamp with a rider on an elephant; lamp-top with two people and two goat/sheep-like animals apparently working a tool — [Kiến Thức](https://kienthuc.net.vn/kho-tri-thuc/doi-song-nguoi-viet-co-qua-nhung-buc-tuong-hon-2000-nam-tuoi-1992845.html)
- [INTERP, contested] Female-figure dagger hilts read as sign of women's high status/"matrilineal" society — same source (popular interpretation)

**Việt Khê boat-coffin (mộ thuyền Việt Khê, M2)**
- [ARCH] Found 1961 at the Việt Khê works site, Ngọc Khê hamlet, Thủy Nguyên, Hải Phòng, together with 4 other boat-coffins in a rice field by the Hàn river; 107 grave goods, mostly bronze, some wood and painted (lacquered) leather; the only Đông Sơn boat-coffin cemetery yielding a Đông Sơn drum; National Treasure — [BTLSQG English pages via search snippet](https://baotanglichsu.vn/en/Articles/1004/16035/national-treasures-the-viet-khe-grave-with-boat-shaped-coffin.html) (page itself 503 during session)
- [ARCH] Bronze item from Việt Khê with a seated khèn-player figure on the handle — [Kiến Thức](https://kienthuc.net.vn/kho-tri-thuc/doi-song-nguoi-viet-co-qua-nhung-buc-tuong-hon-2000-nam-tuoi-1992845.html)

### Inferences
- Đào Thịnh lid is the boldest "funny/sensory" item but needs tasteful handling; best framed as fertility ritual.
- Việt Khê's lacquered leather and wood show perishable crafts survive only in waterlogged boat-coffins — good "what normally rots away" angle.

### Gaps
- Coffin length, wood species and C14 dates for Việt Khê not obtained (museum site down). The "feather fan / sedge mat" grave goods could NOT be verified — do not use without checking.
- Ngọc Lũ weight unconfirmed; full iconography from museum not read.

## 4. Food, dress, houses, farming, trade, casting, music & ritual; story-worthy facts

### Takeaway
Bronze ploughshares in the hundreds (Mả Tre), rice-threshing/pounding figures on drums, khèn-playing statuettes and loincloth/short-skirt dress are archaeologically attested; casting method of drums (lost-wax vs piece-mould) is genuinely debated in the sources found.

### Cited Findings
- [ARCH] Farming: Mả Tre cache dominated by tools — 143 ploughshares, 34 axes, picks, shovels, harvesting knives — [Hoàng Thành Thăng Long](https://hoangthanhthanglong.vn/en/ma-tre-relic-co-loa-commune/)
- [ARCH] Rice processing on drums: people threshing/pounding rice on the Cổ Loa drum — [Archaeology 2016](https://www.anthropology.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/NCKim-Hilgers_VNs-First-City-Arch-Mag_2016.pdf)
- [ARCH] Dress: men in loincloth (khố) with dagger, women in short skirts (Đào Thịnh lid); women's dress details on Làng Vạc dagger hilts — [Kiến Thức](https://kienthuc.net.vn/kho-tri-thuc/doi-song-nguoi-viet-co-qua-nhung-buc-tuong-hon-2000-nam-tuoi-1992845.html)
- [ARCH] Music: 8.5 cm bronze statuette of two people, one carrying the other who plays a khèn (tượng hai người cõng nhau thổi khèn), found at Đông Sơn (Thanh Hóa) by French archaeologists in the early 20th c.; National Treasure — [Kiến Thức](https://kienthuc.net.vn/kho-tri-thuc/doi-song-nguoi-viet-co-qua-nhung-buc-tuong-hon-2000-nam-tuoi-1992845.html); [Lao Động](https://news.laodong.vn/dttg/van-hoa-kien-truc/tuong-hai-nguoi-cong-nhau-thoi-khen-bao-vat-dong-son-doc-ban-o-thanh-hoa-1696054.html)
- [ARCH/INTERP] Lamp held up by kneeling human figures (đèn hình người quỳ); interpreted (not proven) as servants/captives — [Kiến Thức](https://kienthuc.net.vn/kho-tri-thuc/doi-song-nguoi-viet-co-qua-nhung-buc-tuong-hon-2000-nam-tuoi-1992845.html)
- [ARCH] Contact/trade: Chinese coin (~200 BCE) inside the Cổ Loa drum; Chinese-style rammed earth and roof tiles but with Đông Sơn starburst motifs — [Archaeology 2016](https://www.anthropology.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/NCKim-Hilgers_VNs-First-City-Arch-Mag_2016.pdf); glass beads at Làng Vạc — [Báo Nghệ An](https://baonghean.vn/en/bao-vat-duy-nhat-co-o-xu-nghe-10089206.html)
- [INTERP] Casting: sources variously claim lost-wax, piece-mould (clay moulds taken from a decorated model), or both; no peer-reviewed resolution found — [search summary of Smarthistory etc.](https://smarthistory.org/?p=69520) [WEAK]
- [ARCH] Arrowhead production: stone three-piece moulds by the hundreds at Đền Thượng — [Nhân Dân](https://nhandan.vn/mui-ten-dong-co-loa-tu-huyen-thoai-den-lich-su-post504549.html)

**Candidate POV facts (45–60 s), with confidence**
1. Arrowhead factory: ~10,000 arrowheads (93 kg) in a 1 m pit, 3/4 still with casting seams — HIGH (count is estimate).
2. Drum as a piggy bank: Cổ Loa drum buried upside-down 30 cm under a paddy, stuffed with 143 ploughshares, spears, and 19.5 kg of scrap; found by teenagers in 1982 — HIGH (counts differ by source).
3. Black teeth by chemistry: iron + tannin paste, weeks of application, at Đông Xá ~2,000 years ago — HIGH (2026 peer-reviewed).
4. Five million workdays: Kim's estimate for Cổ Loa's early ramparts, built in years-to-decades — MEDIUM (scholarly estimate).
5. The rampart with a tiled roof? Tile layer atop the middle wall — MEDIUM (interpretation).
6. Two-person khèn statuette (8.5 cm), a piggy-back musician — HIGH.
7. Đào Thịnh lid's four couples — HIGH as object; meaning is interpretation.
8. Elephant-rider lamp from Làng Vạc — MEDIUM (popular-press description).
9. Việt Khê "boat" coffin with 107 grave goods and a drum — HIGH.
10. A Chinese coin inside a Vietnamese drum — MEDIUM (only Archaeology magazine).

### Inferences
- Many "daily life" claims (stilt houses, feathered dancers, boats) rely on drum iconography; they are real images but their meaning (ritual vs everyday) is interpretation.

### Gaps
- No direct sources obtained this session for: diet beyond rice (fish, shellfish, buffalo/pig bones), stilt-house depictions on specific drums (Ngọc Lũ/Hoàng Hạ), feathered-headdress dancers, long-distance distribution of drums (e.g. Indonesia), children's burials, accidents/injuries. These are well known in the literature but are NOT verified here; check BTLSQG pages when the site is up, and Higham / Kim (2015, The Origins of Ancient Vietnam).
