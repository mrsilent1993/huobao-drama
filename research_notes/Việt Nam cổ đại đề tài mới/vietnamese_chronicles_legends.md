# Lesser-known pre-938 episodes in Vietnamese chronicles and legend collections (for humorous POV shorts)

## 1. Verification of claims already planned

### Takeaway
Three of the four planned claims are directly verified in TT's classical text: Dương Đình Nghệ's 3,000 "giả tử" (931), Triệu Quang Phục's no-smoke-by-day/dugouts-by-night tactics in Dạ Trạch plus the dragon-claw helmet, and Phùng Hưng's "push buffaloes, wrestle tigers" strength. Khúc Hạo's concrete reforms (lộ/phủ/châu/xã, equal land tax, abolished corvée, household register) and the "khoan dung giản dị" phrasing come from the 19th-c. Cương mục, not TT. TT only says Khúc Hạo held the seat and called himself Tiết độ sứ. Two corrections: the title "Bố Cái Đại Vương" was given to Phùng Hưng posthumously by his son, not taken by Phùng Hưng himself, and the 3,000 were "adopted sons" in TT but "elite soldiers/bodyguard" in CM's translation.

### Cited Findings
**Dương Đình Nghệ – 3,000 adopted sons (931) [CHRONICLE]**
- TT, Ngoại kỷ q.5, year Tân Mão (931), verbatim: "楊廷藝養假子三千人，圖恢復。李進知之，馳以告漢主。是歲廷藝發兵圍進。漢主遣承旨陳寶将兵救之，未至而城陷。進逃㱕。寶至，圍城，廷藝出戰，寶敗死。於是廷藝自稱節度使，領州事。" ("raised 3,000 adopted sons, planning restoration. The Southern Han governor Lý Tiến found out and rode to tell the Han ruler... The city fell before the relief force arrived; Lý Tiến fled; the relief general Trần Bảo besieged it, Đình Nghệ sallied out, Trần Bảo was defeated and killed.") — [TT 外紀卷之五](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之五)
- CM's version (as rendered in the Hawaii translation) describes this as training "3,000 tough soldiers as his bodyguard" and uses the name "Dương Diên Nghệ", citing the Itemized Summaries; it notes VSL/the "Summary Record" calls him Đình Nghệ, from Ái châu (Thanh Hóa) — [KĐVSTGCM 5](https://sites.google.com/hawaii.edu/viettexts/kđvstgcm/kđvstgcm-5)
- VSL: Dương Đình Nghệ was "愛州人" (from Ái châu); it records him attacking 阮進 (sic, = Lý Tiến) who fled home; VSL's surviving text does not mention the 3,000 — [VSL 卷上](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/越史略/卷上)
- CM: after Khúc Thừa Mỹ was captured (923), the Southern Han ruler Lưu Cung wanted to keep Dương Đình Nghệ calm and is quoted with a remark about such people being fit only to be "haltered and harnessed" — [KĐVSTGCM 5](https://sites.google.com/hawaii.edu/viettexts/kđvstgcm/kđvstgcm-5)
- Death: TT year Đinh Dậu (937): "廷藝牙将矯公羡殺廷藝而代之" (his own bodyguard officer Kiều Công Tiễn killed him and took over) — [TT 外紀卷之五](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之五). VSL adds Kiều Công Tiễn was "峯州人" (from Phong châu) and that it happened in spring, 3rd month — [VSL 卷上](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/越史略/卷上)
- CM: Ngô Quyền served as Dương Đình Nghệ's deputy commander, married his daughter, governed Ái châu — [KĐVSTGCM 5](https://sites.google.com/hawaii.edu/viettexts/kđvstgcm/kđvstgcm-5)

**Khúc Hạo's reforms (907–917) [CHRONICLE, late source]**
- TT, year Đinh Mão (907), says only that the Liang named Lưu Ẩn of Guangzhou concurrently Tĩnh Hải governor, "時隐據番禺交州人曲顥據州治，稱節度使，志在相圖" (Lưu Ẩn held Panyu, Khúc Hạo of Giao châu held the provincial seat calling himself Tiết độ sứ, each intending to swallow the other). TT gives no reform list — [TT 外紀卷之五](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之五)
- CM (TB 5/15a), translated: Khúc Hạo designated circuits (lộ), superior prefectures (phủ) and prefectures (châu), appointed district magistrates (chánh lệnh trưởng) and assistants (tá lệnh trưởng); equalized field taxes; abolished corvée (lực dịch); created a household register of names and hometowns overseen by tithing chiefs (giáp trưởng); his rule was "lenience and simplicity", giving people respite — [KĐVSTGCM 5](https://sites.google.com/hawaii.edu/viettexts/kđvstgcm/kđvstgcm-5)
- CM's annotation cites a variant (the "Summary Record", likely VSL or An Nam chí lược) saying he turned each hương (township) into a giáp and set up giáp officials to collect taxes. It also says he served four years before dying, and CM notes the accounts differ — [KĐVSTGCM 5](https://sites.google.com/hawaii.edu/viettexts/kđvstgcm/kđvstgcm-5)
- VSL's surviving text only records "曲顥，梁開平元年，代損為節度使" — [VSL 卷上](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/越史略/卷上)
- Family confusion: TT (Ngô Sĩ Liên) makes Khúc Hạo the father of Khúc Thừa Mỹ, while VSL makes Thừa Mỹ his younger brother — [Wikipedia: Khúc Hạo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kh%C3%BAc_H%E1%BA%A1o)
- A Vietnamese scholarly article (Trần Thị Thái Hà) specifically studies the "bình quân thuế ruộng" policy — [vjol.info.vn](https://vjol.info.vn/index.php/DHSPHN/article/download/83385/71471/)

**Khúc Thừa Dụ's rise (905/906) [CHRONICLE]**
- CM, year Bính Dần (906): Tĩnh Hải military commissioner Khúc Thừa Dụ was given the extra title "Đồng bình chương sự" by the Tang. The Khúc were a powerful local lineage of Hồng châu (today's Bình Giang/Ninh Giang, Hải Dương). He took advantage of disorder to declare himself commissioner, then asked for and got Tang approval. He died in 907 and Hạo succeeded him — [KĐVSTGCM 5](https://sites.google.com/hawaii.edu/viettexts/kđvstgcm/kđvstgcm-5)
- Spy-diplomacy, 917: Khúc Hạo sent his son Thừa Mỹ to Guangzhou as a "goodwill envoy" (歡好使) "to spy out their strengths and weaknesses" (因覘虚實). In 919 Thừa Mỹ asked the Liang, the Southern Han's rival, for the commander's tally and axe (節鉞) and got them. CM says Lưu Cung was enraged by this, and in 923 Thừa Mỹ was captured — [TT 外紀卷之五](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之五); [KĐVSTGCM 5](https://sites.google.com/hawaii.edu/viettexts/kđvstgcm/kđvstgcm-5)

**Triệu Quang Phục at Dạ Trạch (546–548) and dragon claw [CHRONICLE+LEGEND]**
- TT, Đinh Mão (547): "晝則泯絶煙火人跡，夜則以獨木船出兵擊覇先營" (by day, no trace of smoke, fire or people; by night he sent out troops in dugout boats to strike Trần Bá Tiên's camps) — [TT 外紀卷之四](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之四)
- TT describes the swamp: "其澤在朱鳶，周廻不知里數，草木榛莽，叢薄交蔽" (in Chu Diên, circumference of unknown li, choked with brush and thickets). It was reachable only by narrow dugouts, outsiders could get lost and die, and he held it with about 20,000 men — [TT 外紀卷之四](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之四)
- TT, year Bính Dần: the Liang general Trần Bá Tiên (Chen Baxian, later founder of the Chen dynasty) says "我師已老，將士疲勞，且孤軍無援" (our army is worn out, the men exhausted, an isolated force with no support), and his generals fall silent — [TT 外紀卷之四](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之四)
- TT, Kỷ Tỵ (549): "於是得龍爪兜鍪之瑞，用以擊賊" (he obtained the auspicious dragon-claw helmet and used it against the enemy). The note says: "俗傳澤中神人褚童子，時乘黃龍自天而下，脫龍爪付王" (folk tradition: the swamp spirit Chử Đồng Tử came down from heaven on a yellow dragon, pulled off a dragon claw and gave it to the king). The chronicle itself flags this as hearsay (俗傳) — [TT 外紀卷之四](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之四)

**Phùng Hưng (c. 766/791) [CHRONICLE + LEGEND]**
- TT, year Tân Mùi (791): "馮興豪富有勇力，能排牛搏虎" (wealthy, with brave strength, could push over buffaloes and grapple tigers). The Tang protector-general Cao Chính Bình "為政重歛" (ruled with heavy exactions). Phùng Hưng, from Đường Lâm, besieged the protectorate "用本鄕人杜英翰計" (on the plan of fellow-villager Đỗ Anh Hàn), and "正平憂憤成疾，發背死" (Cao Chính Bình fell ill from worry and rage and died of an abscess on his back) — [TT 外紀卷之五](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之五)
- The title: "子安尊為布盖大王，俗謂父曰布，母曰盖，故以為名焉" (his son An honored him as Bố Cái Đại Vương; folk speech calls father "bố" and mother "cái", hence the name). TT also says the Phùng brothers had earlier styled themselves "Đô Quân" and "Đô Bảo" — [TT 外紀卷之五](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之五)
- VĐUL (as quoted by Đại biểu Nhân dân): Phùng Hưng could fight tigers and wrestle buffaloes. His brother Phùng Hãi could carry 1,000 cân and carry a boat holding 1,000 hộc on his back for 10 dặm. After Hưng died, many wanted to make Hãi ruler, but the chieftain Bồ Phá Cần (Henry: Bồ Phá Lặc) insisted on Hưng's son Phùng An — [Đại biểu Nhân dân](https://daibieunhandan.vn/viet-su-giai-thoai-bo-cai-dai-vuong-phung-hung-10039041.html)

### Inferences
- "3,000 giả tử" is safe to use with the TT citation. Adopting a private army as "sons" was a late-Tang/Five Dynasties warlord practice, so it is historically plausible and not a mistranslation. Comic premise: being "son #2,417" of a man you've never met.
- The Khúc Hạo reform list should be presented as "Cương mục (19th c.) records…" rather than "Toàn thư says…". The quoted Vietnamese phrase "chính sự cốt chuộng khoan dung, giản dị, nhân dân đều được yên vui" matches CM's content (the Hawaii English is "lenience and simplicity"), so it is most likely the Viện Sử học Vietnamese translation of CM.
- Phùng Hưng's tyrant opponent dying of a stress abscess is a ready-made punchline, and it is in the chronicle.

### Gaps
- I could not access a Vietnamese-language edition of TT or CM, so the exact Vietnamese wording "nuôi 3000 con nuôi" and "chính sự cốt chuộng khoan dung…" is not quoted from a page. The Chinese originals and English CM above support the content.
- The CM translation's "3,000 tough soldiers" came via a summarizing fetch. Check the CM Hán text, which may itself say 假子.
- The VĐUL Phùng Hãi figures (1,000 cân, 10 dặm) come from a newspaper retelling, not a primary translation.

## 2. Lesser-known episodes (15) with sources, details and confidence

### Takeaway
Pre-938 sources hold plenty of absurd, ironic, everyday-perspective material beyond the famous legends. The best chronicle-backed items are Tinh Thiều's surname snub (541), the Lý-household tax hike that triggered Đinh Kiến's revolt (687), Dương Thanh's "promotion" (819), Phùng Hưng's opponent dying of stress (791), Khúc Hạo's spy-envoy son (917), and Nhã Lang's dragon-claw swap (570). The best legends are Lý Ông Trọng's faked dysentery and the hollow bronze statue, the White Pheasant embassy that got lost on the way home, the Fish Demon crowing like a rooster, the tree demon killed during a circus show, and Cao Biền's string of failed spirit-suppressions.

### Cited Findings

**E1. Tinh Thiều (Bính Thiều 并韶) – rejected for having an "unknown surname" (541) [CHRONICLE]**
- TT, Tân Dậu (541): Tinh Thiều, "富於詞藻" (rich in literary talent), went to the Liang capital seeking office. The Liang minister of personnel Sái Tôn "以并姓無前賢，除廣陽門郎" (because no worthy had ever borne the surname Tinh, appointed him gatekeeper-official of the Quảng Dương gate). "韶耻之，還鄕里，從帝謀起兵" (Thiều was ashamed, went home, and joined Lý Bí in planning revolt) — [TT 外紀卷之四](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之四)
- POV idea: the gate-guard post as a career; a rejected scholar becomes a co-founder of a state.

**E2. Lý Bí founds Vạn Xuân (544) [CHRONICLE]**
- TT, Giáp Tý (544): "建國號曰萬春，望社稷至萬世也。起萬壽殿以爲朝會之所" (named the state Vạn Xuân, "Ten Thousand Springs", hoping the dynasty would last ten thousand generations; built Vạn Thọ hall for court). It lasted about 4 years before Trần Bá Tiên's counter-attack, and in 548 (Mậu Thìn) Lý Bí died of illness in a cave — [TT 外紀卷之四](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之四)
- VSL calls him 阮賁 (Nguyễn Bí): "阮賁反，據龍編城，自稱南越帝，置百官，改元天德，國號萬春" — [VSL 卷上](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/越史略/卷上)
- Chùa Khai Quốc (later Trấn Quốc): not in the TT Giáp Tý entry. The "founded under Lý Nam Đế" claim is temple tradition (see Gaps).

**E3. Dạ Trạch guerrilla life (546–550) [CHRONICLE+LEGEND]**: see section 1. POV idea: a soldier who may not light a cooking fire all day, and a lost Liang scout.

**E4. Lý Phật Tử, Nhã Lang and the swapped dragon claw (557–571) [CHRONICLE+LEGEND]**
- TT, Đinh Sửu (557): after a truce dividing the land, "佛子有子雅郎，求婚王女杲娘。許之。遂成姻好" (Lý Phật Tử's son Nhã Lang asked to marry Triệu Việt Vương's daughter Cảo Nương; accepted) — [TT 外紀卷之四](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之四)
- Nhã Lang tells his wife: "昔吾两父王爲讐，今爲婚姻不亦善乎" (our two fathers used to be enemies; now we're married, isn't that nice?). "雅郎潜謀易其爪" (he secretly plotted to swap the claw), then "雅郎歸，與其父謀，襲王取其國" (went home, plotted with his father, attacked the king and took his country) — [TT 外紀卷之四](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之四)
- End, TT 571 (Tân Mão): "越王引馬奔至大鴉海口，阻水歎曰：「吾窮矣！」遂投于海" (fled on horseback to the Đại Nha estuary, blocked by water, sighed "I'm finished!" and threw himself into the sea) — [TT 外紀卷之四](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之四)
- Ngô Sĩ Liên moralizes: "婦人謂嫁曰歸，則夫家迺其家也" (a married woman's home is her husband's), i.e. blaming Cảo Nương's divided loyalty — [TT 外紀卷之四](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之四)
- Note: this is structurally a doublet of the Mỵ Châu–Trọng Thủy story (already used), which is worth flagging in the video.

**E5. Đinh Kiến's revolt over a tax hike (687) [CHRONICLE]**
- TT, Đinh Hợi (687), full entry: "嶺南俚戶舊輸半課，都護劉延祐使之全輸。俚戶始怒謀作亂。李嗣先為謀主，延祐殺之。其餘黨丁建等合眾圍府城。城中兵少不支，嬰壘待援。廣州大族馮子由幸立功，按兵不出。建殺延祐。後桂州司馬曹直静攻建，殺之。" Paraphrase: Lý (Li) households had always paid half-tax. Protector-general Lưu Diên Hựu made them pay full. Lý Tự Tiên plotted and was killed. His followers under Đinh Kiến besieged the city. The garrison was too small and hunkered down awaiting relief. The great Guangzhou clan head Phùng Tử Do, hoping to grab credit, kept his troops idle. Đinh Kiến killed Lưu Diên Hựu, and was later killed by Tào Trực Tĩnh of Quế châu — [TT 外紀卷之五](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之五)

**E6. Mai Thúc Loan, the "Black Emperor" (722) [CHRONICLE, brief]**
- TT, Nhâm Tuất (722): "賊帥梅叔鸞㨿州，稱黑帝，外結林邑、真臘人等，眾號三十萬" (claimed 300,000 followers, allied with Champa and Chenla). The Tang sent the eunuch general Dương Tư Húc with Quang Sở Khách to crush him — [TT 外紀卷之五](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之五)
- The well-known folk details (lychee-carrying porters, dark skin) are not in this TT entry, so treat them as legend.

**E7. Phùng Hưng, Bố Cái Đại Vương (791) [CHRONICLE+LEGEND]**: see section 1. Key comic beats: the tyrant dies of a stress boil; his son invents a title that literally means "Great King Dad-Mom"; the strongman uncle Phùng Hãi is passed over.

**E8. Dương Thanh kills Lý Tượng Cổ (819) [CHRONICLE]**
- TT, Kỷ Hợi (819): Protector-general Lý Tượng Cổ, a Tang imperial clansman, "以貪縱苛刻失眾心" (lost support through greed and harshness). He distrusted Dương Thanh, a hereditary local chief and former Hoan châu prefect, so he "召為牙將" (summoned him to be a bodyguard officer) and then ordered him to campaign against the Hoàng Động people. "清因人心忿怒，夜還襲州，陷之，殺象古" (Thanh, riding popular anger, turned back at night, took the city, killed Tượng Cổ). Afterwards the Tang general Quế Trọng Vũ failed to defeat him, Lý Nguyên Gia tried to lure him "and he didn't come" (誘之，不來), and the Hoàng Động people brought in Champa raiders — [TT 外紀卷之五](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之五); VSL also records "為府人楊清所殺" — [VSL 卷上](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/越史略/卷上)

**E9. Khúc Thừa Dụ / Khúc Hạo / Khúc Thừa Mỹ (905–923) [CHRONICLE]**: see section 1. Comic beats: a self-appointed governor who then politely asks the dying Tang for approval and gets it; the "goodwill ambassador" who is openly a spy; Thừa Mỹ asks the wrong dynasty for his badge of office and gets captured for it.

**E10. Kiều Công Tiễn (937–938) [CHRONICLE]**: Dương Đình Nghệ's own bodyguard officer (牙將) killed him. Kiều Công Tiễn was from Phong châu and asked the Southern Han for help, which brought on Bạch Đằng — [TT 外紀卷之五](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之五); [VSL 卷上](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/越史略/卷上); [KĐVSTGCM 5](https://sites.google.com/hawaii.edu/viettexts/kđvstgcm/kđvstgcm-5). The irony is that the head of the "3,000 sons" was killed by his own bodyguard.

**E11. Lý Ông Trọng (Lý Thân), the giant sent to Qin (late 3rd c. BCE setting) [LEGEND]**
- LNCQ narrative 11: Lý Thân of Từ Liêm was "two yards and three feet tall", rough and violent, and once killed a man. Hùng Vương could not bring himself to execute him. An Dương Vương sent him to Qin Shi Huang as a tribute gift. Qin made him Ty Lệ commandant and posted him at Lâm Thao, and the Xiongnu dared not approach. He was titled "Vạn Tín hầu" and allowed to go home — [Henry, Book Two](https://www.rajraf.org/10282/uploads/article/1261/4_Eric_Henry.pdf)
- When Qin summoned him again, he hid in the forest. An Dương Vương claimed he had died "of dysentery". When Qin sent an inspector, An Dương Vương "had a soup made and poured into a pond to serve as evidence". Qin demanded the corpse, so Lý Thân committed suicide. His body was rubbed with mercury and shipped. Qin cast a bronze statue at the Sima Gate of Xianyang whose belly held "more than a dozen people", who moved it to scare Xiongnu envoys — [Henry, Book Two](https://www.rajraf.org/10282/uploads/article/1261/4_Eric_Henry.pdf)
- Later, Tang governor Triệu Xương dreamed of Lý Thân lecturing on the Zuozhuan and built him a temple. Cao Biền restored it at Thụy Hương village, Từ Liêm. VĐUL 2.2 has a parallel version with fewer fantastic details — [Henry, Book Two](https://www.rajraf.org/10282/uploads/article/1261/4_Eric_Henry.pdf)
- Embellishment: "Ruan Wengzhong" (阮翁仲) is a Chinese legend of a giant Qin general whose bronze statue stood at Xianyang. The Vietnamese tale grafts a local origin onto it (my inference, based on the Chinese "Wengzhong" statue tradition; not verified in this session).

**E12. The White Pheasant (Bạch trĩ) tribute embassy (Zhou King Cheng, c. 11th c. BCE setting) [LEGEND]**
- LNCQ narrative 10: Hùng Vương's envoys bring a white pheasant to the Zhou court, but needed a chain of several interpreters ("a series of translations") before the Duke of Zhou understood. The Duke asked why they cut their hair short, tattoo themselves, go bareheaded and have curled toes. Answers: short hair for the forest, dragon tattoos so water dragons won't bite, curled toes for climbing trees, bare heads against the heat, betel to "suppress bad odours and blacken our teeth". They came because there had been three years without storms or big waves, "so … a sage must now exist in China". The Duke refused the gift ("no benefit conferred, no reward claimed"). Then "the Emissaries forgot where the return route lay", so he gave them a chariot and pointed them south. Coda: Confucius left Văn Lang out of the Spring and Autumn Annals as too uncivilized — [Henry, Book Two](https://www.rajraf.org/10282/uploads/article/1261/4_Eric_Henry.pdf)
- Embellishment: the core (Yuèshāng 越裳 envoys, multiple interpreters, white pheasant, south-pointing chariot) is a well-known Chinese story (Shangshu dazhuan / Hou Hanshu) relocated to Văn Lang (general scholarly knowledge; not fetched this session).

**E13. Ngư tinh – Fish Demon who crowed like a rooster (Hồng Bàng setting) [LEGEND]**
- LNCQ narrative 2: a fish demon "more than fifty yards long … many feet, like those of a centipede" lived in a cave at Fish-Demon Crag. It took human form and traded salt, rice and axes. Immortals came one night to dig a canal for boatmen, but the demon turned into a white rooster and crowed, so they thought dawn had come and flew off, leaving the unfinished "Buddha Road". Lạc Long Quân (variant: Đào Thương Long) pretended to feed it and threw a red-hot piece of steel into its mouth, then chopped it up. The tail became Bạch Long Vĩ, and the head turned into a dog and became "Cẩu Đầu" island — [Henry, Book Three](https://www.rajraf.org/10282/uploads/article/1262/5_Eric_Henry_final_2.pdf)

**E14. Hồ tinh – nine-tailed fox of West Lake (Hồng Bàng setting) [LEGEND]**
- LNCQ narrative 3: a thousand-year nine-tailed fox in a cave under a stone hill west of the future capital disguised itself as a "White-Clothes" mountain dweller from Tản Viên. It joined their singing groups and lured boys and girls into its cave. Lạc Long Quân had his water forces smash the hill, creating "Thi Hồ trạch" ("Fox-Corpse Swamp" = Hồ Tây). Place names that came from it: Hồ Thôn (Fox Village), Lỗ Hồ đàm, and the Thiên Niên quán temple built to keep it down — [Henry, Book Three](https://www.rajraf.org/10282/uploads/article/1262/5_Eric_Henry_final_2.pdf)

**E15. Mộc tinh – tree demon killed during an acrobat show (setting Kinh Dương Vương → Qin → Đinh) [LEGEND]**
- LNCQ narrative 4: a giant chiên đàn (sandalwood) tree at Bạch Hạc, "a thousand levels high", became the demon Xương Cuồng. It demanded a human sacrifice every last day of the year, and locals outsourced the victim by having Bà Lộ people seize a Lao person. Qin commandant Nhâm Ngao banned the sacrifices, so the demon killed more people and the sacrifices resumed. Finally, in Đinh Tiên Hoàng's time, an 80-plus-year-old northern magician, Du Văn Mâu, staged an annual acrobatics show: rope-walkers, pole-climbers, a man in a bamboo fish-trap cage, horse-riders picking up objects at a gallop, children with gongs. When the demon came down to eat the offerings and watch the tricks, he beheaded it. The performer names use 尚 for "thằng" (lowly fellow), per Nguyễn Thị Oanh — [Henry, Book Three](https://www.rajraf.org/10282/uploads/article/1262/5_Eric_Henry_final_2.pdf)
- Caveat: the climax is set in the Đinh era (968–979), so it is pre-938 only for the backstory.

**E16 (bonus). Cao Biền vs. the local spirits (864–868) [LEGEND around a historical figure]**
- Tô Lịch (LNCQ 19): Cao Biền meets a silver-haired old man boating. "I am Lịch of the Tô lineage … I live here in this river." The man claps his hands and vanishes, and the river gets its name — [Henry, Book Three](https://www.rajraf.org/10282/uploads/article/1262/5_Eric_Henry_final_2.pdf)
- Long Đỗ (LNCQ 19 and 26): a two-yard figure in gold robe and purple cap hovers over the river. In a dream it says: "I am the Spirit Long Đỗ … I heard that someone came here and built a city wall … Even if you should try to make me disappear, it would not worry me in the slightest." Cao Biền spends three days of spells and buries gold, silver, bronze and iron amulets (LNCQ 26: a 1,000-cân iron effigy). A storm turns them to "flying ashes". He sighs: "This place is ruled by Spirits … I must return to the North" — [Henry, Book Three](https://www.rajraf.org/10282/uploads/article/1262/5_Eric_Henry_final_2.pdf)
- Tản Viên (LNCQ 15): during Cao Biền's grisly suppression ritual, the mountain spirit rides past on a white horse and spits at him — [Henry, Book Three](https://www.rajraf.org/10282/uploads/article/1262/5_Eric_Henry_final_2.pdf)
- Cao Lỗ (LNCQ 35): the crossbow-maker's spirit politely appears in a dream to announce he is leaving, because An Dương Vương was a Gold Rooster spirit, the Lạc lords White Monkeys, and he a White Dragon: "Roosters and Monkeys get along well, but there is tension between those creatures and Dragons" — [Henry, Book Three](https://www.rajraf.org/10282/uploads/article/1262/5_Eric_Henry_final_2.pdf)
- Sĩ Nhiếp (LNCQ 23, a real 2nd–3rd c. governor): when Lâm Ấp raiders opened his grave around 160 years after his death, the body was undecayed. "Terrified at this, they hurriedly reburied him." Cao Biền later chats with a stranger about the Three Kingdoms, and the stranger vanishes at Sĩ Nhiếp's grave — [Henry, Book Two](https://www.rajraf.org/10282/uploads/article/1261/4_Eric_Henry.pdf)
- Historical counterweight: CM's Imperial Appraisal calls Cao Biền a rebel official ("What is there about him to praise?") and disputes the old history's canal-dredging story and his credit for Đại La citadel — [KĐVSTGCM 5](https://sites.google.com/hawaii.edu/viettexts/kđvstgcm/kđvstgcm-5). TT's Ngô Sĩ Liên comments "高駢鑿港之役，何其異耶" (how strange the canal-cutting affair) — [TT 外紀卷之五](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/大越史記全書/外紀卷之五)

## 3. Source reliability and academic debate

### Takeaway
Everything pre-938 in Vietnamese chronicles is late: VSL is 14th c., TT 1479/1697, CM 19th c. The Bắc thuộc entries are mostly drawn from Chinese histories, with occasional folk notes that TT itself marks as 俗傳 ("popularly transmitted"). LNCQ is a 14th–15th-century compilation, and Kelley argues its Hồng Bàng material is a medieval invented tradition. That is contested in Vietnam, so legends should be framed as "the 14th–15th c. tale says…".

### Cited Findings
- Henry dates LNCQ tales to an "unknown, 14th century" author, with some (Long Độ, Đô Lỗ) marked 15th century. He calls the first three historical legends "of a wildly improbable nature" and the last two as having "some relationship to real events" — [Henry, Book Two](https://www.rajraf.org/10282/uploads/article/1261/4_Eric_Henry.pdf); [Henry, Book Three](https://www.rajraf.org/10282/uploads/article/1262/5_Eric_Henry_final_2.pdf)
- Henry suggests the gruesome Cao Biền ritual in the Tản Viên tale reads "as if a piece of anti-Chinese propaganda got inserted into the tale … by accident", while other Cao Biền tales portray him favorably — [Henry, Book Three](https://www.rajraf.org/10282/uploads/article/1262/5_Eric_Henry_final_2.pdf)
- Kelley, "The biography of the Hồng Bàng clan as a medieval Vietnamese invented tradition", Journal of Vietnamese Studies 7(2), 2012, pp. 87–130. Argument: earlier Vietnamese and Chinese sources never mention the Hùng kings or Văn Lang, and the Lạc Long Quân legend is absent from VSL and An Nam chí lược. The view is contested; Nhân Dân ran rebuttals — [search summary incl. Cambridge/JSEAS & others](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-southeast-asian-studies/article/mythographical-journey-to-modernity-the-textual-and-symbolic-transformations-of-the-hung-kings-founding-myths/4444E56BF953F891ADB9FB2FC4E790CE)
- Translations of LNCQ: Lê Hữu Mục (1960), Đinh Gia Khánh–Nguyễn Ngọc San (most widely circulated), Nguyễn Thị Oanh (critical edition, NXB KHXH 2024) — [Wikipedia: Lĩnh Nam chích quái](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C4%A9nh_Nam_ch%C3%ADch_qu%C3%A1i)
- Inter-chronicle discrepancies to flag on screen: Khúc Hạo as Thừa Mỹ's father (TT) vs brother (VSL); Dương Đình Nghệ vs Dương Diên Nghệ (CM); Lý Bí vs "Nguyễn Bí" (VSL); Lý Phật Tử vs "Nguyễn Phật Tử" (VSL) — [Wikipedia: Khúc Hạo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kh%C3%BAc_H%E1%BA%A1o); [VSL 卷上](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/越史略/卷上); [KĐVSTGCM 5](https://sites.google.com/hawaii.edu/viettexts/kđvstgcm/kđvstgcm-5)
- CM's Imperial Appraisal attributes the 938 Bạch Đằng victory partly to luck ("because of luck"). That is a funny, citable historiographical aside even though Bạch Đằng itself is excluded — [KĐVSTGCM 5](https://sites.google.com/hawaii.edu/viettexts/kđvstgcm/kđvstgcm-5)

### Inferences
- Safe framing per category: "Toàn thư (1479) chép…" for chronicle items, "Truyện trong Lĩnh Nam chích quái (TK 14–15) kể…" for legends, and "Cương mục (TK 19) chép thêm…" for Khúc Hạo's reforms.
- Chinese-name variants (Nguyễn vs Lý) in VSL reflect a later taboo on the Lý surname (a commonly cited explanation; not verified here).
